## Design Context

### Users
Tubestr/Nook is a private family video app with two primary audiences.

- Children use the kid-facing side to watch family videos, capture new clips, and make simple edits.
- Parents use Parent Zone to manage identity, family connections, approvals, moderation, and safety settings.

The core job is to make family video sharing feel safe, warm, and easy without exposing kids to algorithmic feeds, ads, or confusing controls.

### Brand Personality
The product should feel warm, private, and reassuring.

- Kid-facing spaces should feel playful, creative, and inviting.
- Parent-facing spaces should feel calm, trustworthy, and in control.
- The overall voice should stay human, family-oriented, and non-technical.

### Aesthetic Direction
Use a dual-mode visual system with one shared brand foundation.

- Kid mode: soft warmth, creative energy, obvious next steps, and strong prompts toward capture and editing.
- Parent mode: quieter control-room feel, reduced decoration, clearer hierarchy, denser information, and more restrained typography.

Avoid generic AI-interface fingerprints such as repetitive frosted cards, decorative blobs everywhere, concentric empty-state illustrations, and one-size-fits-all chrome reused across every screen.

### Design Principles
1. Make the next action obvious within two seconds.
2. Separate kid energy from parent control; do not force one visual voice across both.
3. Use decoration sparingly; hierarchy should come from layout, typography, and emphasis first.
4. Keep copy calm, specific, and non-technical, especially in safety and error states.
5. Empty states should teach the product and immediately point toward capture, editing, or connection.
